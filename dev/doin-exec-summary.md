# Executive Summary

Communities across the United States are investing billions of dollars in broadband infrastructure, digital inclusion programs, workforce development, and technology-enabled services. Yet policymakers and practitioners continue to face a fundamental challenge: understanding which interventions actually improve digital opportunity and under what conditions. Too often, decisions are guided by static reports, fragmented datasets, and delayed evaluations that provide limited insight into how policies perform in real-world environments. The Digital Opportunities Intelligence Network (DOIN) addresses this challenge by transforming digital equity policy from a reporting exercise into a continuous learning process.

The **Digital Opportunities Intelligence Network (DOIN)** is a next-generation decision-support and policy-learning system designed to help communities, researchers, and policymakers understand, evaluate, and improve digital opportunity outcomes. Built upon the Digital Opportunities Compass framework, DOIN expands the focus of digital equity beyond infrastructure deployment to include the broader factors that influence opportunity, including local context, governance, digital skills, applications, and social and economic outcomes.

## The DOIN Framework

DOIN consists of three integrated components:

### 1. The Compass

The Compass provides a rigorous measurement framework for understanding digital opportunity across six dimensions:

- Contexts
- Governance
- Connectivity
- Skills
- Applications
- Outcomes

Rather than focusing solely on broadband availability, the Compass provides a holistic view of the conditions that enable individuals and communities to benefit from digital technologies.

### 2. The Network

The Network serves as the connective layer of the system by linking research, infrastructure data, community assets, stakeholder knowledge, program information, and policy interventions. Using knowledge graph technologies, DOIN transforms fragmented datasets into a shared intelligence infrastructure capable of revealing relationships that traditional reporting systems often overlook.

### 3. The Intelligence Layer

The Intelligence Layer applies causal reasoning, predictive analytics, and evidence synthesis to support decision making. Instead of simply reporting what happened, it helps communities understand why outcomes occurred and what actions are most likely to be effective moving forward. This transforms policy from a reactive process into a continuous cycle of learning and improvement.

Together, these capabilities form a dynamic **Policy Learning Machine** that continuously observes conditions, interprets signals, evaluates interventions, measures outcomes, and refines recommendations over time. Rather than asking only where broadband exists, DOIN seeks to answer a more meaningful question:

> **What actions are most likely to improve digital opportunity in this specific community, and how can we learn from the results?**

# Why DOIN Should Be Open Source

A foundational principle of this proposal is that DOIN should be developed as **open-source public infrastructure**.

Digital opportunity is a public-interest challenge that affects economic development, workforce preparedness, education, healthcare access, and civic participation. The intelligence systems used to guide these investments should therefore be transparent, collaborative, and accessible to the communities they serve.

Open-source development provides several benefits:

- **Transparency:** Communities can understand how recommendations are generated and how evidence is evaluated.
- **Trust:** Public visibility into methods, models, and assumptions increases confidence in decision-making processes.
- **Collaboration:** Researchers, practitioners, nonprofits, governments, and community organizations can contribute improvements and innovations.
- **Local Adaptation:** Communities can customize the framework to address unique regional needs and contexts.
- **Public Value:** Knowledge generated from publicly funded research and public investments remains a shared resource rather than a proprietary asset.
- **Long-Term Sustainability:** Open governance reduces dependence on a single vendor or institution and allows the system to evolve through collective stewardship.

# Expected Impact

DOIN is designed to help communities:

- Improve the effectiveness of broadband and digital equity investments.
- Connect infrastructure deployment to measurable social and economic outcomes.
- Identify local barriers to adoption, skills development, and participation.
- Evaluate the effectiveness of interventions using evidence rather than assumptions.
- Share knowledge across regions and learn from comparable communities.
- Build long-term community capacity through continuous policy learning.

# Vision

The long-term vision for DOIN is to create a shared learning infrastructure for the digital economy—one that connects data, research, local knowledge, and evidence-based decision-making into a continuously improving system.

DOIN is not envisioned as another dashboard, database, or reporting platform. It is envisioned as a community-centered intelligence network that helps communities move beyond measuring digital inequities toward systematically learning how to overcome them. By combining measurement, intelligence, and collaborative governance, DOIN seeks to help communities make better decisions, allocate resources more effectively, and achieve more equitable digital outcomes.
